Scope: Whisperio iOS/iPadOS/watchOS/macOS App Store app (`ai.whisperio.mobile`) + Electron desktop.

## (a) Data flow map

| Engine / feature | Leaves device | To whom | Terms that apply |
|---|---|---|---|

| Replicate | audio (uploaded via `/v1/files`) | api.replicate.com | Replicate |

| iCloud history sync (`storageMode` default `.iCloud`) | history records | user's PRIVATE CloudKit DB `iCloud.ai.whisperio.mobile` | Apple |

Never leaves: API keys (Keychain, `WhenUnlockedThisDeviceOnly`, not in iCloud Keychain; only sent as auth to their own provider), typed keyboard text, settings, vocabulary. Cloud engines are gated by `cloudConsentGranted` plus a key (`SettingsStore.swift` ~L153-170).

## (b) Does anything reach our server?

No. The developer operates no server; no analytics SDK, no telemetry (grep: no third-party endpoints in the mobile app other than the providers above, huggingface.co, api.github.com).
Exception class: the desktop app's update check calls GitHub's public releases endpoint (GitHub sees IP/UA/version). CloudKit is Apple's infrastructure in the user's own iCloud account; the developer cannot read it. TestFlight/App Store analytics come from Apple, not us.
Verified 2026-10-16: CloudKit records (`CD_RecordingEntity`, `CD_DigestEntity`) carry transcripts and metadata only (`segmentsData`/`speakerNamesData` are diarization JSON, no `CKAsset`, no audio bytes); audio files stay in the device sandbox. The policy now says so.

### App Privacy label recommendation
"Data Not Collected" is defensible. Apple defines "collect" as transmitting data off device in a way that lets the developer and/or third-party partners access it for longer than needed to service the request. The developer receives nothing; the user's own CloudKit private DB is not accessible to the developer; BYO providers are the user's own service relationship, not developer-integrated third-party partners; Apple's on-device speech is on-device by default. Residual risk: Apple's guidance also counts data sent to third parties the app integrates. A strict reading could argue the app transmits audio to providers (a reviewer might ask). Honest fallback if asked, all "linked to user = No, tracking = No, purpose = App Functionality": Audio Data (Other User Content / Audio Data) and "User Content > Other" for cloud STT/cleanup; optional. Recommendation: set Data Not Collected, keep the policy, review notes and listing explicit that audio goes to the user's chosen provider (done). Revisit if a default cloud provider, bundled key, analytics or any developer server is ever added.

## (f) Terms of use

Apple's standard EULA (Licensed Application End User License Agreement) is sufficient and is the default. A separate Terms page is not required for a free, accountless, BYO-key app. Only add a short "Terms" section if a concrete need appears: (1) a disclaimer that third-party AI output may be inaccurate and the user is responsible for provider charges and compliance with provider terms (nice-to-have; could be one paragraph in privacy.html), (2) user-supplied GitHub token responsibilities, (3) any paid feature or developer-run service. If added, keep it consistent with PolyForm Noncommercial (source) and TRADEMARKS.md (name/logo).

## Legal basis for the texts changed on 2026-10-16 (verified quotes)
Fetched on 2026-10-16 from the official sources named below (EUR-Lex via a browser session; Apple pages via the developer site). Quotes are verbatim.

### Apple App Review Guidelines — https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/
- **5.1.1(i)** (privacy policy link + content; basis for the in-app "Privacy Policy" link and for the rewritten policy): "All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app in an easily accessible manner. The privacy policy must clearly and explicitly: Identify what data, if any, the app/service collects, how it collects that data, and all uses of that data. Confirm that any third party with whom an app shares user data […] will provide the same or equal protection of user data […]. Explain its data retention/deletion policies and describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion of the user's data."
- **5.1.1(ii)** (consent + purpose strings; basis for the microphone/speech usage-string rewrite and the cloud consent sheet): "Apps that collect user or usage data must secure user consent for the collection […]. Apps must also provide the customer with an easily accessible and understandable way to withdraw consent. Ensure your purpose strings clearly and completely describe your use of the data."
- **5.1.1(iii)** (data minimisation; basis for keeping on-device speech as the default): "Apps should only request access to data relevant to the core functionality of the app and should only collect and use data that is required to accomplish the relevant task."
- **5.1.2(i)** (third-party AI disclosure + explicit permission; basis for the consent sheet naming the provider and the provider list in the policy): "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so."
- **5.1.2(ii)**: "Data collected for one purpose may not be repurposed without further consent unless otherwise explicitly permitted by law."
- **3.1.1** (why no IAP is needed for a free BYO-key app — nothing is unlocked by the key, the key is the user's own service credential, not a Whisperio feature unlock): "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app […] you must use in-app purchase. Apps may not use their own mechanisms to unlock content or functionality, such as license keys […]". Note: a user's provider API key does not unlock Whisperio functionality; it authenticates the user to a third-party service the user pays separately. [[DO WERYFIKACJI]] whether App Review treats BYO keys as acceptable under 3.1.1 — no written Apple statement found; precedent only.
- **2.5.4** (background modes; basis for the review-notes justification of `audio` and `remote-notification`): "Multitasking apps may only use background services for their intended purposes: VoIP, audio playback, location, task completion, local notifications, etc."
- **4.4.1** (keyboard extension; basis for the policy's Full Access paragraph and the review notes): keyboards must "Remain functional without full network access and without requiring full access" and must not "Launch other apps besides Settings".
- **2.3.1** (basis for removing the unsupported "Anthropic" provider from the mobile listing): "Don't include any hidden, dormant, or undocumented features in your app; your app's functionality should be clear to end users and App Review."

### EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689
- **Art. 50(1)** (basis for the AI notice on LLM digests/summaries): "Providers shall ensure that AI systems intended to interact directly with natural persons are designed and developed in such a way that the natural persons concerned are informed that they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious from the point of view of a natural person who is reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect, taking into account the circumstances and the context of use."
- **Art. 50(2)**: "Providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content, shall ensure that the outputs of the AI system are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated. […] This obligation shall not apply to the extent the AI systems perform an assistive function for standard editing or do not substantially alter the input data provided by the deployer or the semantics thereof […]" — Whisperio's cleanup/rewrite of the user's own dictation is an assistive editing function; the daily digest is a summary generated by the user's chosen model for the user alone.
- **Art. 50(4)** (text published to inform the public — not Whisperio's case, the output is private to the user): "Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates text which is published with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest shall disclose that the text has been artificially generated or manipulated."
- **Art. 50(5)**: "The information referred to in paragraphs 1 to 4 shall be provided to the natural persons concerned in a clear and distinguishable manner at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure."
- **Art. 113** (application): "It shall apply from 2 August 2026." — i.e. in force at the time of this release.
- Role note: Whisperio is a client that lets the user call a model with the user's own account; the provider of the AI system is OpenAI/Groq/… [[DO WERYFIKACJI]] whether Whisperio is a "deployer" (Art. 3(4)) when the end user operates it on their own behalf for a non-professional activity (Art. 2(10) excludes natural persons using AI "in the course of a purely personal non-professional activity"). The in-app AI notice is added regardless, as good practice and for 5.1.2(i).

### GDPR, Regulation (EU) 2016/679 — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679
- **Art. 4(7)**: "'controller' means the natural or legal person […] which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data"; **Art. 4(8)**: "'processor' means a natural or legal person […] which processes personal data on behalf of the controller". Whisperio's developer runs no server and receives no personal data, so is neither controller nor processor for the user's recordings; the user's chosen provider processes data under the user's own contract with it — this is the basis for the policy's section 9 ("Your rights").
- **Art. 13(1)(a),(c),(e)** and **13(2)(a),(b),(c)** (information duties where data IS collected — listed so the policy mirrors them for the local data the app holds and the recipients the user selects): identity and contact details of the controller; purposes and legal basis; "the recipients or categories of recipients of the personal data, if any"; "the period for which the personal data will be stored"; the rights of access, rectification, erasure; the right to withdraw consent. The policy's sections 3, 4, 9 and 11 cover these for the data on the device and the user-selected recipients.
