Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 17, 2026

TL;DR. Whisperio runs on your device. We — the developer of Whisperio — do not run any server, do not collect any data (Google Play's Data safety form uses a broader definition; see section 9), and do not see your recordings, transcripts, or settings. There is no account, no login, no ads, no analytics and no telemetry. On Apple platforms, dictation uses Apple's on-device speech recognition by default. If you choose a cloud provider (OpenAI, ElevenLabs, Replicate, Deepgram, AssemblyAI, Groq, Mistral, or a server of your own) and enter your own API key, your audio is sent directly from your device to that provider. Optional iCloud sync (Apple platforms only) uses your private iCloud database, which we cannot read. On Android there is no on-device recognition and no sync: audio goes only to the provider you pick, after you consent in the app (see sections 8 to 10).

1. Who we are

Whisperio is a dictation app developed by Daniel Kasprzyk. It is an app for iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch (to be distributed through the App Store, bundle ID ai.whisperio.mobile; coming soon) and a desktop app for Windows, macOS and Linux. A Mac App Store version is coming. Whisperio for Android (version 1.0.0, application ID ai.whisperio.mobile) is being prepared for Google Play. The source code is published at github.com/draenger/whisperio under the PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0 (source-available, not for commercial use).

This policy describes how Whisperio handles data on your device and where data goes when you use its optional features. It does not cover the websites or APIs of third-party providers you choose to use through Whisperio.

2. Data Whisperio does not collect

Whisperio has no backend server operated by us. We do not have, see, or store:

  • Your audio recordings
  • Your transcripts
  • Your settings, hotkeys, or vocabulary
  • Your API keys
  • Your IP address or device identifiers
  • Crash reports, usage analytics, or telemetry of any kind

There is no account, no login, and no registration. We use no advertising or tracking SDKs. We have no way to contact you or identify you unless you contact us directly.

3. Data that stays on your device

The following information is stored locally on your device:

  • Settings — hotkeys, theme, provider choice, custom vocabulary, output preferences. Stored in the app's sandbox, private app storage (Android) or local config directory.
  • API keys — keys you enter for the providers below. On iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch (and in the Mac App Store app, once released) they are stored in the Apple Keychain, readable only when the device is unlocked and not synced through iCloud Keychain. On the desktop app they are stored in the operating system's secure credential store (Keychain on macOS, Credential Manager on Windows, libsecret on Linux) where available; otherwise in the app's local config directory. On Android they are encrypted with an AES-256-GCM key held in the Android Keystore (see section 8).
  • Dictation history — transcripts, notes, optional AI summaries and, if you keep them, the audio recordings. You can delete individual entries, delete all audio, or erase everything from the app (Settings → Storage on iOS; in Settings (storage and retention), via "Erase all data", on Android).
  • Downloaded local models — optional speech and language models (Whisper / WhisperKit and GGUF models) that you download inside the app. They are downloaded from Hugging Face (Hugging Face Privacy Policy); that request reveals your IP address to Hugging Face but no audio or text. After download, these models run entirely on your device.

You can locate and remove this data manually:

  • iOS / iPadOS / Apple Watch: data lives inside the app's sandbox container — deleting the app from the device removes it completely (API keys in the Keychain are removed with it; if you also used iCloud sync, see section 5).
  • macOS (Mac App Store app, once released): inside the app's sandbox container under ~/Library/Containers. macOS (desktop app): ~/Library/Application Support/Whisperio
  • Android: data lives in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read. Use "Erase all data" in the app, or uninstall Whisperio, to remove it completely (see section 8).
  • Windows: %APPDATA%\Whisperio
  • Linux: ~/.config/Whisperio

4. Data sent to third parties

4.1 Speech recognition

  • Apple speech recognition (default on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch; the Mac App Store app, once released, will use the same default). By default Whisperio asks Apple's Speech framework to recognize speech on the device only; audio does not leave your device. If you turn on "allow online recognition" in Settings, or if a language is not available on-device and you permit it, Apple may process the audio on its servers under Apple's Privacy Policy. Whisperio itself never receives that audio.
  • Android. Whisperio for Android 1.0.0 has no on-device speech recognition and does not use Android's system speech recognizer, so every dictation needs one of the cloud providers below (or your own server).
  • Local models (Whisper / WhisperKit, llama.cpp-based language models) — processed entirely on your device; nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Cloud providers, only if you select one and enter your own API key. Whisperio then sends your recorded audio directly from your device to that provider, over HTTPS, so it can return a transcript. Your custom vocabulary is sent to the provider together with the audio (as a prompt or as keyterms, depending on the provider, where the provider supports it) to improve recognition. On Android the available providers are OpenAI, Groq, Mistral AI, ElevenLabs, Deepgram, AssemblyAI and a self-hosted server; Replicate is not available on Android. We are not a party to that exchange and receive no copy. Providers:
    • OpenAI — Privacy Policy, API data controls. OpenAI states that data sent to its API is not used to train its models unless you opt in.
    • ElevenLabs — Privacy Policy. ElevenLabs may use content to train its models unless you opt out in your ElevenLabs account ("Data use" setting).
    • Replicate — Privacy Policy and Terms. Audio is uploaded to Replicate to be processed by the model you chose.
    • Deepgram — Privacy Policy and Terms. Deepgram may use content to train its models unless you opt out.
    • AssemblyAI — Privacy Policy and Terms. AssemblyAI may use customer data to train its models unless you opt out.
    • Groq — Privacy Policy and Services Agreement. Groq states it does not use inputs or outputs to train models.
    • Mistral AI — Legal terms.
    • A self-hosted or third-party OpenAI-compatible server (e.g. whisper.cpp, faster-whisper, LocalAI, or another provider you point Whisperio at) — data is sent only to the URL you configured. Connections to the official providers always use HTTPS. For a self-hosted or custom URL on a private network (for example localhost, a home or office LAN address, or a .local name) the app also allows plain HTTP, which is not encrypted in transit; use it only on a network you trust, or use an HTTPS address. The privacy of that server is governed by whoever operates it (which may be you).
    Each provider's own terms and privacy policy apply to data you send it, including whether it is retained or used for training; some of them train on content by default unless you opt out in your own account with them. Check your provider's settings before sending sensitive audio. No cloud provider is used until you select it and enter a key, and the app asks for your consent before first sending audio to a cloud provider (on Android, nothing is uploaded before you give that consent).

4.2 AI cleanup and summaries (optional)

If you enable the optional AI cleanup, rewrite or daily-summary features, your transcript text (not audio) is sent to the language-model provider you configured for that feature (on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch, and in the Mac App Store app once released: OpenAI, or an on-device model; on the desktop app also Anthropic and Replicate), subject to that provider's privacy policy (for Anthropic: Privacy Policy and Commercial Terms, under which Anthropic states it may not train models on customer content). On-device models and Apple Intelligence (where available) process text on your device. Android 1.0.0 has none of these features; it only applies a local, rule-based tidy-up on your device.

4.3 Sync to GitHub (optional, off by default)

This feature is not available on Android. If you turn on "Sync to GitHub" and enter a personal access token, repository and branch, Whisperio uploads your transcripts, journal entries and summaries as Markdown files to your own GitHub repository, directly from your device, under GitHub's privacy statement. If that repository is public, anyone can read the files. The token is stored in the Keychain. Turn the feature off or delete the token to stop; files already uploaded remain in your repository until you delete them there.

4.4 Apple Watch and keyboard

  • Apple Watch: the watch records audio and sends it to your paired iPhone, which transcribes it using the engine you selected (section 4.1). The watch does not contact any provider on its own.
  • Android keyboard: see section 8.
  • Whisperio keyboard (iOS): the keyboard works as a normal keyboard without "Allow Full Access". Full Access is requested only so that the microphone key can hand the recording to the Whisperio app through the shared App Group and, if you selected a cloud provider, reach that provider. Whisperio does not log or transmit what you type, and we receive nothing through the keyboard.

5. iCloud sync

iCloud sync does not exist on Android; Android history stays on the device. On iPhone and iPad (and, once released, the Mac App Store app), Whisperio can sync your dictation history between your devices using Apple CloudKit, in your private iCloud database (container iCloud.ai.whisperio.mobile). What is synced: transcripts, their metadata (date, duration, category, speaker labels) and daily summaries; the audio recordings themselves stay on the device that made them and are never uploaded to iCloud by Whisperio. This data is stored by Apple in your iCloud account under Apple's Privacy Policy; we operate no server in this path and cannot read, access or recover it. Sync is used when you are signed in to iCloud; you can switch Whisperio to on-device-only storage in Settings (storage / sync mode), or disable iCloud for Whisperio in iOS Settings → [your name] → iCloud. API keys are not synced.

To delete synced data, use "Erase all data" in the app, or iOS Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage → Whisperio → Delete Data. Deleting the app alone does not remove data already stored in iCloud.

6. Updates and GitHub (desktop app)

The desktop app checks for new releases by contacting GitHub's public release endpoint. This request includes only what a normal HTTPS request reveals (your IP address, user agent, app version) and is handled by GitHub under GitHub's own privacy policy. Whisperio does not transmit any personal data, recordings, or settings during this check. You can disable update checks in Settings. The App Store apps (iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch, and the Mac version once released) and the Android app do not check for updates themselves; updates come through the App Store or Google Play.

7. Permissions, TestFlight and App Store

On Android Whisperio requests only two permissions: microphone (RECORD_AUDIO) and internet (INTERNET). It requests no storage, notification, contacts, location or foreground-service permissions.

On Apple platforms Whisperio asks for access to the microphone (to record your voice) and speech recognition (for Apple's recognizer). It uses background audio so a dictation in progress can finish when the screen locks, and silent CloudKit notifications for iCloud sync; it sends no push notifications and does not track you across apps or websites. You can withdraw any permission at any time in system Settings.

If you install a beta through Apple TestFlight, Apple may collect crash logs, device information, and feedback you submit through TestFlight itself; this flows to Apple under Apple's Privacy Policy and is shown to the developer in aggregate. Likewise, if you allow Apple to share analytics with developers in your device settings, Apple may provide us anonymous crash and usage statistics. Whisperio does not add any analytics SDK of its own.

If you allow your device to share usage and diagnostics with Google, Google Play may provide us crash and app-not-responding reports (Android vitals) through the Play Console, under Google's Privacy Policy. Whisperio does not add any crash-reporting SDK of its own. On Android you can withdraw the microphone permission at any time in Android Settings → Apps → Whisperio → Permissions.

8. Whisperio for Android

This section describes what is specific to Whisperio for Android (version 1.0.0). The rest of this policy applies to Android as well, except where it says a feature is Apple-only or desktop-only.

8.1 When audio is recorded and where it goes

  • Whisperio records audio only while you are dictating: after you tap the microphone button in the keyboard, in the Quick Settings tile, in the "Dictate" launcher shortcut, or in the app. There is no background recording and no foreground service; leaving the screen you started from, or hiding the keyboard, stops the recording.
  • The recording is sent directly from your device, over HTTPS (except the private-network case below), to the provider you selected, using your own API key, and only after you have given explicit consent in the app (shown during setup). Nothing is uploaded before that consent. We do not receive a copy.
  • Your custom vocabulary is sent to the provider together with the audio, as a prompt or as keyterms (for providers that support it).
  • If you point Whisperio at a self-hosted or custom server on a private network, the app may use plain HTTP for that address (see section 4.1). Official providers are always contacted over HTTPS, and the app does not follow redirects from a plain-HTTP address.
  • The only network destinations are the endpoints of the providers you configured (including key checks when you save a key) and your own custom or self-hosted URL. The Android app contains no Whisperio server, no account system, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising SDK, and no Google Play Services or Firebase dependency. It is free and has no in-app purchases.

8.2 API keys

Your API keys are stored encrypted. They are encrypted with an AES-256-GCM key that is held in the Android Keystore and cannot be exported from it; only the encrypted data is written to the app's private storage. Keys are excluded from Android backup and from device-to-device transfer (the app disables backup and declares data-extraction rules that exclude all app data), so they are not copied to a cloud backup or to a new phone; on a new device you enter your keys again. If the Keystore key is lost or invalidated, Whisperio deletes the stored ciphertext and asks you to paste the key again. Keys are not written to logs, history or error messages.

8.3 Keyboard

The Whisperio keyboard on Android is a dictation panel. It does not store or transmit what you type. On your device it only checks the field type (to detect password fields) and the single character before the cursor (to decide whether to add a space); the only data it sends anywhere is the audio you record with its microphone button. Dictation is disabled in password fields, so a spoken password never reaches a provider. In fields that ask keyboards not to learn from input (the Android "no personalized learning" flag), the transcript is inserted but is not saved to your history.

8.4 History and deletion

Your dictation history (transcripts and, if you keep them, the audio files) is stored only on your device, in the app's private storage. In Settings you can choose whether to keep audio, set automatic deletion after 1, 7 or 30 days, delete all audio, or use "Erase all data" to remove history, audio, keys and settings. Uninstalling Whisperio deletes everything it stored. There is no cloud sync on Android. A transcript copied to the clipboard by the Quick Settings tile or the shortcut is an ordinary clipboard entry, which the system and other apps may be able to access according to your Android version and settings. You can turn on "Treat transcripts as sensitive" to mark these clipboard entries as sensitive, which hides them from clipboard previews.

8.5 Permissions

Microphone (to record when you tap the microphone button) and internet (to reach your chosen provider). Nothing else is requested.

9. Google Play "Data safety" and the App Store privacy label

Our store listings can look different at first sight. In the App Store, Whisperio's privacy label says "Data Not Collected". In Google Play's Data safety section, Whisperio for Android declares that it collects audio (voice or sound recordings) and your custom vocabulary (as user-generated content), and that it does not share them. Both describe the same behavior; the two stores define the words differently.

  • Google defines collection by whether data leaves the device. In Google's words (Play Console Help, "Provide information for Google Play's Data safety section"): "'Collect' means transmitting data from your app off a user’s device." Because Whisperio for Android sends your recording and vocabulary off your device to the provider you chose, Google's definition counts it as collected, even though Whisperio's developer never receives it.
  • Google defines sharing as follows: "'Sharing' refers to transferring user data collected from your app to a third party." We declare "not shared" under Google's exemption for "Transferring user data to a third party based on a specific user-initiated action, where the user reasonably expects the data to be shared": you tap the microphone button, with your own key, after consenting in the app. We have not declared the data as processed "ephemerally", because once the provider receives it, retention is under the provider's control and may outlast the request (see section 4.1).
  • Apple's label is about what the developer and its partners collect. Whisperio's developer does not receive or have access to your data, so on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch the label reads "Data Not Collected". On both stores the underlying fact is the same: your audio goes to the provider you pick and is not available to us.

Data sent to official providers is encrypted in transit (HTTPS); see section 4.1 for the plain-HTTP exception for private-network custom servers. Whisperio holds no data on a server and has no accounts, so there is no account to delete; use "Erase all data" or uninstall to remove local data, and contact your provider about data it holds.

10. GDPR (RODO): who is responsible for what

This section is for users covered by the EU General Data Protection Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (official text on EUR-Lex; the quotations below were copied from gdpr-info.eu, an unofficial reproduction of that text). It explains our analysis of the roles and is not legal advice.

10.1 What the developer processes

We run no server and receive no audio, transcripts, vocabulary, keys or identifiers from the app (section 2). We therefore do not process the personal data you dictate, and have no such processing for which we would need a legal basis under Article 6, which provides: "Processing shall be lawful only if and to the extent that at least one of the following applies:". If you contact us by email or GitHub issue, Daniel Kasprzyk (contact: section 14) is the controller of what you send. We use it only to answer you, on the basis of our legitimate interest in responding to inquiries (Article 6(1)(f)), and delete emails within 12 months after the matter is closed unless longer retention is needed for legal claims. GitHub issues are public and also processed by GitHub under its own privacy statement. You may request access, rectification, erasure, restriction or object (Articles 15–18, 21), and you have the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority (Article 77); in Poland this is the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (Prezes UODO, ul. Stawki 2, 00-193 Warszawa, uodo.gov.pl), or the authority where you live or work.

10.2 Roles

Article 4(7) defines the controller as the body "which, alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data"; Article 4(8) defines the processor as one "which processes personal data on behalf of the controller". When you dictate with a cloud provider, the recording goes from your device to the provider you chose, under your own account and API key and under that provider's terms. The developer of Whisperio does not receive the audio and does not decide how the provider processes it. Our analysis is therefore that, for the audio and vocabulary you send to a provider, the developer is not a controller of the processing the provider performs (and is not its processor either); you choose that provider and contract with it directly, and it is for that provider, according to its own role, to meet the GDPR obligations for its own processing. Read the provider's privacy policy and data-processing terms (links in section 4.1) before sending sensitive audio. If a supervisory authority or court sees the roles differently in a particular case, their view prevails.

10.3 Household exemption

Article 2(2)(c) says the Regulation does not apply to processing "by a natural person in the course of a purely personal or household activity". That may describe your own use if you dictate for purely personal purposes, but not if you dictate for professional or business purposes. Whether it applies is a question about your own use. We do not read it as relieving the provider that receives the audio of its own obligations, and we do not rely on it to describe our own role.

10.4 Why this policy exists

Article 13(1) provides: "Where personal data relating to a data subject are collected from the data subject, the controller shall, at the time when personal data are obtained, provide the data subject with all of the following information:", beginning with "the identity and the contact details of the controller". Even though we do not receive your data, we publish this policy so that it is clear who does what: the developer is identified in section 1 and can be contacted as shown in section 14. Your rights as a data subject (access, deletion and so on) are exercised with the party that holds the data (for emails you send us, that is us; see 10.1); see section 12.

11. Children

Whisperio is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect any data from anyone, regardless of age.

12. Your rights and retention

Because Whisperio does not store your personal data on any server we control, requests for access, correction, or deletion of personal data under GDPR, CCPA, or similar laws are limited to data held by the third-party providers you configured (and by Apple for iCloud), except emails or issues you send us, which we keep for up to 12 months after the matter is closed and for which you can contact us directly (section 10.1). Please contact those providers directly to exercise your rights with respect to data they hold; retention periods are set by them. The roles of the different parties under the GDPR are explained in section 10.

For data stored on your device, you have full control: you can view, edit, export, or delete it through the app or by removing the files listed in section 3. Data is kept on your device until you delete it or delete the app. You can revoke consent to cloud providers at any time by removing the API key or switching engine in Settings.

If this policy changes materially, the "Last updated" date at the top will change and a notice will appear in the app's release notes. Older versions are visible in the project's Git history at github.com/draenger/whisperio.
